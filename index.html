Treatment options

Match the system to the water—not the other way around.

Whole-home treatment addresses water entering the house. A separate point-of-use system may be appropriate when the main objective is drinking and cooking water.

01

Whole-home carbon filtration

Designed for taste, odor and disinfectant reduction. Media, service flow and certification must match the water and home.

02

Water softening

Reduces hardness minerals associated with scale, spotting and soap inefficiency. Recommended after hardness is measured.

03

Reverse osmosis drinking water

Point-of-use treatment for customers seeking an additional barrier for drinking and cooking water.

04

Private-well treatment

Custom treatment based on certified laboratory results, well-pump capability and the property’s plumbing conditions.

Common questions

Straight answers about home water treatment.

Does every Denver home need a softener?
No. Denver’s water is generally softer than water in several surrounding communities. Hardness should be measured at the property and evaluated against the customer’s scale and spotting concerns.
Is a whole-home filter the same as a softener?
No. Carbon filtration addresses selected tastes, odors and disinfectants. A softener exchanges hardness minerals. Some homes may benefit from one system, both systems or neither.
Can you size a system over the phone?
An intake call can identify a preliminary equipment category and service-flow target. Final selection requires verification of water quality, pressure, flow, plumbing and installation conditions.
What about lead, PFAS or other health concerns?
Health-related concerns require appropriate testing and equipment certified for the specific reduction claim. Taste, odor and TDS readings cannot establish whether water is safe.
Do private wells require different treatment?
Yes. Private-well treatment should be based on a certified laboratory analysis and the well pump’s available flow—not a municipal-water average.
